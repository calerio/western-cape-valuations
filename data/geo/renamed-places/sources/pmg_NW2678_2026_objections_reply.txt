Minister of Sport, Arts and Culture
 Question NW2678 to the Minister of Sport, Arts, and Culture
	Share this page:
    18 May 2026 -
NW2678
Van Zyl, Ms A M
        to ask the
        Minister of Sport, Arts, and Culture
(1). 	Whether, with reference to concerns raised regarding geographical name change processes affecting Robert Sobukwe Town (formerly Graaff‑Reinet), Xamdebo (formerly Aberdeen), Bishop Limba (formerly Adendorp), Ekhephini (formerly Barkly East) and KuGompo City (formerly East London), responses have been issued to the more than 38 000 objections submitted in respect of the specified name changes which were gazetted on 6 February 2026; if not, why not; if so, what the details of such responses?
(2). 	Whether his department has complied with the provisions of the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act, Act 3 of 2000 regarding the communication of administrative decisions within a reasonable period; if not, why not; if so, what timelines did his department set for the finalisation of the name change process and the communication thereof to affected communities?
(3). 	Whether his department will reconsider and/or review the decisions taken in light of public objections and concerns regarding the gazetting and objection process; if not, why not; if so, what are the relevant details?
(4). 	What steps has his department taken to ensure that future geographical name change processes (a) comply with the requirements of the South African Geographical Names Council Act, Act 118 of 1998 and (b) provide for meaningful public participation?
Source
Link
Reply:
(1).
The geographical name changes referred to above were approved by the Minister following recommendations by the South African Geographical Names Council (SAGNC) and were published in the Government Gazette on 6 February 2026. These approvals constitute final administrative decisions in terms of the South African Geographical Names Council Act, 1998 (Act No. 118 of 1998) (SAGNC Act).
The Department received tens of thousands of objections within the prescribed one-month objection period, which closed on 6 March 2026.
Due to the unprecedented volume, complexity, and varying formats of the objections received, the Department is currently undertaking a structured process to record, consolidate, analyse, and consider all valid objections.
Individual responses have not yet been issued to objectors. The objections are presently being processed through an internal data processing system to ensure accuracy, consistency, and procedural integrity.
The Department will communicate the outcomes of the objection consideration process, together with reasons for its decisions, to affected parties in accordance with the Promotion of Administrative Justice Act, 2000 (Act No. 3 of 2000) (PAJA) and the SAGNC Act, once the consideration process has been finalised.
(2).
The Department remains committed to full compliance with PAJA. The initial administrative decisions were published in the Government Gazette on 6 February 2026, thereby fulfilling the publication requirements prescribed by the SAGNC Act.
The Department is currently engaged in a procedurally fair process of considering the substantial number of objections received following publication. Given the unprecedented volume and complexity of the objections, the process necessarily requires adequate time to ensure proper consideration of all valid submissions.
Affected parties will, in due course, be provided with adequate notice of the final outcomes, together with reasons for the decisions taken, as contemplated in section 5 of PAJA. The Department is processing and assessing objections in accordance with the constitutional and administrative law principles of lawfulness, reasonableness, and procedural fairness as envisaged in PAJA.
Once the objection consideration process has been finalised, outcomes will be communicated to affected parties in accordance with section 10(5) of the SAGNC Act. Objections submitted after gazetting do not invalidate the decisions taken or the procedures already undertaken but rather form part of the process provided for in the SAGNC Act.
(3).
All objections received within the prescribed timeframe are being carefully considered as part of the Department’s ongoing administrative process in accordance with the provisions of the SAGNC Act and PAJA.
Any decision to confirm, amend, vary, or withdraw the original approvals will be taken in accordance with the applicable legislative and constitutional requirements governing lawful, reasonable, and procedurally fair administrative action.
Upon completion of the current administrative processing phase, the matter may be referred to the SAGNC for consideration and recommendations to the Minister regarding whether the approved geographical names should be upheld, amended, or withdrawn.
Affected parties will be informed of the outcomes in due course in accordance with the applicable statutory prescripts. Parties who remain aggrieved following the finalisation of the administrative process retain their right to seek appropriate judicial review relief before the High Court.
(4).
(a)
 The Department remains fully committed to complying with the SAGNC Act. All processes are conducted with oversight by Provincial Geographical Names Committees and the SAGNC. Lessons learned from the current process are being applied to strengthen future compliance.
(b)
 The Department continues to improve its public participation mechanisms, including broader and earlier consultations through provincial structures, clear public notices, and accessible platforms for public input, in line with PAJA and best practice.
